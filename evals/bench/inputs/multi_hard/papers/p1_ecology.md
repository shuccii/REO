# Urban green corridors increase bird species richness (fictional abstract)

We surveyed breeding birds at 24 sites in one metropolitan area: 12 sites inside green corridors and 12 sites in conventional urban parks. Each corridor site was surveyed by point counts for a total of 10 hours over the season; each park site was surveyed for a total of 2 hours, because park access was restricted. Observers were trained with the same protocol, and species identification was verified with audio recordings. Corridor sites had a mean richness of 31.4 species, compared with 17.9 species in parks (Welch t-test, p < 0.001). Habitat structure (canopy cover, shrub density) was measured at every site with the same methods. We conclude that green corridors support substantially more bird species than conventional parks and recommend corridor construction as a conservation strategy.
