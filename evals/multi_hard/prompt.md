# Urban green corridors increase bird species richness (fictional abstract)

We surveyed breeding birds at 24 sites in one metropolitan area: 12 sites inside green corridors and 12 sites in conventional urban parks. Each corridor site was surveyed by point counts for a total of 10 hours over the season; each park site was surveyed for a total of 2 hours, because park access was restricted. Observers were trained with the same protocol, and species identification was verified with audio recordings. Corridor sites had a mean richness of 31.4 species, compared with 17.9 species in parks (Welch t-test, p < 0.001). Habitat structure (canopy cover, shrub density) was measured at every site with the same methods. We conclude that green corridors support substantially more bird species than conventional parks and recommend corridor construction as a conservation strategy.
</paper>

<paper name="p2_clinical.md">
# A pilot trial of breathing training for chronic tension-type headache (fictional abstract)

Patients were recruited from a headache clinic when they attended because of a recent worsening of symptoms. Forty patients received eight weeks of guided breathing training. Headache severity was rated on a validated 0–10 scale at enrolment and after eight weeks by assessors who followed a standard interview script. Mean severity fell from 7.2 to 4.6 (paired t-test, p < 0.001), and 60% of patients were responders (≥ 30% reduction). In a historical cohort from the same clinic, collected from routine follow-up visits, only 20% of patients met the responder definition over a similar period. Breathing training is therefore an effective treatment for chronic tension-type headache.
</paper>

<paper name="p3_fatigue.md">
# Fatigue life of a laser-welded steel joint (fictional abstract)

We tested 18 laser-welded joints in constant-amplitude axial fatigue at stress ranges of 220, 180 and 140 MPa (six specimens per level, stress ratio R = 0.1). All specimens failed between 4 × 10⁴ and 9 × 10⁵ cycles. A Basquin S–N curve fitted to the data (R² = 0.96) was used to estimate the stress range corresponding to a life of 10⁹ cycles, which gave 61 MPa. Specimen dimensions and weld geometry were measured before testing and were within tolerance. We recommend 61 MPa as the design stress range for ultra-high-cycle loading of this joint in railway bogies.
</paper>

<paper name="p4_kinetics.md">
# Kinetics of the hydrolysis of ester E in aqueous buffer (fictional abstract)

The hydrolysis of ester E was followed by UV absorbance at 25.0 °C in phosphate buffer (pH 7.4). Runs were performed at initial ester concentrations of 0.10, 0.20 and 0.40 mM, each in triplicate, and the absorbance was converted to concentration with a calibration curve measured on the same day. The half-life was 12.1 ± 0.3 min and did not depend on the initial concentration. This constant half-life confirms that the hydrolysis is second order in ester, and we report a second-order rate constant derived from the half-life measured at 0.10 mM.
</paper>

<paper name="p5_acoustics.md">
# Measuring the speed of sound with a resonance tube (fictional abstract)

The speed of sound in air was determined from the first two resonance lengths of a closed tube driven by a 512 Hz tuning fork, which removes the need for an end correction. Room temperature was 25 °C, as read from a calibrated thermometer, and the measurement was repeated ten times. The mean speed of sound was 343.2 ± 0.4 m/s (mean ± standard error). This agrees with the theoretical value for dry air at the measurement temperature, confirming the accuracy of the method.
</paper>

<paper name="p6_fraud.md">
# Gradient boosting for credit-card fraud detection (fictional abstract)

We trained a gradient-boosting classifier on 284,000 credit-card transactions, of which 0.9% were fraudulent. The data were split chronologically: the first nine months for training and the last three months for testing, so that no future information was used. Hyperparameters were tuned with cross-validation inside the training period. On the test period, the model achieved an accuracy of 99.1%. We conclude that the model detects fraud reliably and is ready for deployment.
</paper>
