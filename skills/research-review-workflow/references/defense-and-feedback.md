# Defense questions and constructive feedback

## Critique without intimidation

Use demanding, specific, respectful questions about evidence and reasoning. Do not mimic harassment, humiliating speech or an instructor's personality. First explain the author's strongest supported contribution. Distinguish fatal-to-this-claim defects, substantial repairs, missing evidence and presentation improvements. A missing artifact is not automatically misconduct or proof the underlying research is false.

For each actionable finding use:

`ID / affected claim & location / severity / confirmed or suspected / evidence / mechanism & consequence / feasible correction / rerun or measurement / acceptance condition`.

Critical: a demonstrated issue destroys a central inference or evaluation and propagates to dependent claims. Major: a material gap or issue requires qualification or repair before the scoped claim is defensible. Minor: a local correction leaves the main inference intact. Severity concerns impact; certainty concerns evidence. Do not convert a suspicion into a confirmed defect. Record strength, scope and missing evidence for all consequential conclusions.

A revision plan states priority, dependency, estimated cost/time if knowable, required evidence and a concrete done condition. Offer a wording limitation when new experiments are not feasible, without disguising unverified conclusions. Extra experiments must address the actual question, not create a new thesis by default.

## Question bank

Generate task-specific questions anchored to claims, pages, figures or designs, not an unrelated generic list. Include likely multidisciplinary questions and at least these reasoning levels when relevant:

| Level | Main probe | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Comprehension | What problem and measurable goal does the work address? | Which result directly answers it? |
| Choice | Why this method/assumption rather than the simplest alternative? | What happens when it is removed or violated? |
| Validity | What evidence makes this inference warranted? | Could a batch effect, proxy or another mechanism give the same result? |
| Limits | Which materials/conditions/populations does this cover? | What boundary case would break it? |
| Contribution | What differs from the closest prior study? | Is the contribution an algorithm, validation, new insight or application? |
| Next step | Which experiment best separates competing explanations? | What would each outcome make you retract or retain? |

Record each question's purpose, evidence needed, plausible follow-up and answer boundary. An answer outline states the conclusion, inspected evidence, assumption/limitation and next verification. Mark unanswered points; never supply invented numerical results or unverified approval. If no specific claim can be grounded, label a generic planning prompt rather than assign a fictitious claim ID.

## Interactive rehearsal

Ask one main question at a time and let the user answer. Judge their answer against inspected artifacts: correct facts, directness, supporting evidence, assumptions/limits and clarity. Ask a follow-up tailored to the actual answer before providing a concise improved outline. If the answer admits uncertainty honestly, preserve that admission and suggest the next check. Continue within the user's requested session/time limit. A prepared question bank is appropriate if interactive rehearsal was not requested.

For interim presentations, distinguish present evidence, planned work and the feasibility of reaching the thesis objective. For final defenses, probe end-to-end consistency, contribution, independent validation and limitations. Use the actual institution's accessible criteria; inaccessible internal rubrics remain unconfirmed. Do not predict passing or claim the actual committee will ask these exact questions.
