# Local context, continuity and domain knowledge

Use when reviewing ongoing user research or a workspace with `RESEARCH_REVIEW_CONTEXT.md`. Keep personal paths, institutional rules and unpublished findings in the local adapter, outside the distributed plugin. A user can override the configured scope for a particular task.

## Read before judging

1. Read configured current-state and known-issues records, relevant original teaching feedback, and the most recent relevant earlier reviews (normally up to three; expand only for an unresolved thread). Match research topic and artifact version: reviews of other projects do not establish defects in this project.
2. Read the stage-relevant institution criteria and laboratory presentation guidance. Keep official degree criteria, laboratory conventions, historical examples and AI recommendations separately attributed. Record source version, date, applicability and uncertainty. Historical grading thresholds are not current requirements without confirmation.
3. Read the configured domain reference's relevant sections, their provenance and limitations. Use definitions and domain checks to generate useful questions. Retrieve the primary methods/results or raw data before consequential scientific conclusions; a local synthesis does not inherit full-text access from another agent.

Record read/partial/unavailable for these inputs in the source ledger. If mandatory records are unavailable, continue a clearly scoped preliminary review, flag incomplete context, and leave continuity/domain-dependent conclusions unassessed. No automatic waiting for faculty or authentication. When no local adapter exists, use supplied history and current artifacts; state that no local context was configured, do not invent a vault or rules.

## Track improvement rather than rediscovering it

Maintain a readable table: prior finding ID/source and affected claim; previous artifact version; current artifact/locator; change; status; remaining work and acceptance evidence.

- **New**: no matching issue in inspected history. This means new within inspected history, not proof nobody noticed it before.
- **Known unresolved**: previously identified and still supported by current evidence.
- **Partially improved**: a relevant change is inspected but its acceptance condition remains unmet.
- **Resolved**: new evidence demonstrates the earlier acceptance condition; preserve prior issue and its provenance.
- **Recurred**: inspected history demonstrates an earlier resolution and current evidence demonstrates the same defect again.
- **Unverified**: latest artifact, baseline, previous result or acceptance evidence could not be checked. An unchecked checklist box is not proof that a defect persists.

Known risks and suspected concerns remain distinct from confirmed defects. Negative benchmark results are findings under specified conditions, not automatically methodological errors. Do not repeat historical numbers as current without checking the original data/version. If the history says an interval is invalid solely because its variance is constant, inspect its actual derivation and calibration: constancy alone does not invalidate an interval. Similarly, descriptor IDs and small-sample SHAP require context-sensitive evaluation rather than automatic universal rejection.

Map unresolved/partially improved/recurred issues to open formal findings. Map resolved issues only when the existing report contract's independent correction-evidence requirements are satisfied. The narrative history status does not bypass validator gates. Stable IDs should remain stable across reviews when possible.

## Apply local knowledge to the actual task

Use laboratory presentation conventions to evaluate communication within their stated context; do not turn a slide-count preference into a scientific defect or degree failure. Distinguish issues fixable by clearer wording from missing evidence requiring reanalysis or experiment. For domain references, distinguish measured quantity from proxy and compare conditions, material families, units, assumptions and independent validation. Cite what was inspected, and expose missing primary evidence. Domain knowledge guides questions; it does not establish that this user's model or material obeys a particular mechanism.
