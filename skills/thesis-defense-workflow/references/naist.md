# NAIST criteria for the master's thesis defense

Checked on 2026-10-16 from the public page [NAIST degree criteria](https://www.naist.jp/campuslife/degree/criteria.html). Re-check the page and the current program handbook before each use; this file is a summary, not the authority.

## Current public criteria (all programs)

The master's thesis is assessed as a scholarly paper in the field, with emphasis on novelty and usefulness, against six items (original Japanese, then a working translation):

| ID | Original | Working translation | What to prepare |
|---|---|---|---|
| N1 | 研究の背景や目的が十分に説明されている | Background and objectives are sufficiently explained | General problem → usual approaches → what is specific here → objective whose verb the results can satisfy |
| N2 | 研究の進め方や研究方法についての吟味が十分になされている | The approach and methods were sufficiently examined | Why this method, data and conditions; the alternatives considered; arbitrary thresholds justified |
| N3 | 実験データや理論計算の結果、調査結果についての整理と解析が十分になされている | Experimental, computational or survey results are well organized and analyzed | Each figure traceable to data/conditions; uncertainty, repeats or error bars; fair baselines |
| N4 | 得られた結果に基づく結論や新しい仮説への展開が論理的に十分に示されている | Conclusions or new hypotheses follow logically from the results | Objective → result → conclusion map; no conclusion stronger than its evidence; origin of any new hypothesis |
| N5 | 論文が学術的な記述法で適切に記述されている | The thesis is written in appropriate academic form | Structure, terminology, citation correctness and coverage, figure and table quality |
| N6 | 研究倫理の問題に適切に対応している | Research-ethics issues are handled appropriately | Citation and reuse, data handling and retention, authorship and acknowledgments, consent/permissions where relevant |

How it is decided (as stated on the page): each committee member evaluates every item; the student passes when each member's evaluation meets the set standard; results go to the faculty meeting after the thesis presentation (thesis review and examination). The page gives **no points or numeric thresholds**. The page also says committee members check the capstone; the rubric itself is internal (see below).

Doctoral theses use the same six items, with the prerequisite that the main part is published or to be published in peer-reviewed venues, and a public hearing (preliminary review) precedes the final examination.

## Milestones, capstone and schedule (official page, checked 2026-10-16)

NAIST treats the degree as the capstone and sets milestones before it.

| Course | Milestone | Capstone |
|---|---|---|
| Master's | at least one interim report by November of year 2 | thesis review in February of year 2 |
| Doctoral | one interim report by November of each year | thesis review in February of year 3 |

A rubric for milestones and the capstone is available in the internal education-record system (教育カルテ). The page asks students to use it regularly to improve their own work. The agent cannot see it: ask the student to read it and record the items in the fact sheet.

Master's schedule on the page:

| Period | Event |
|---|---|
| December–January | Application for thesis review and thesis abstract, submitted through the education-record system by the deadline |
| January–February | Faculty meeting approves the thesis title and committee |
| mid to late February | Master's thesis presentation (thesis review and examination) |
| late February or early March | Faculty meeting: report, deliberation and completion decision |

The page states no presentation or Q&A length. Talk length, Q&A length, format and language vary by program and year: confirm them with the program office or advisor.

## Other official documents worth checking

Reported from the 2026 student handbook and library pages by a secondary research summary; open the official documents to confirm before relying on them:

- Completion requires passing the thesis review and the final examination after the required research supervision (completion requirements page).
- Each student has two or more supervisors from different laboratories, one of them the main supervisor; the thesis title is declared and the thesis submitted with the main supervisor's approval (course regulations in the student handbook). In practice, advisor approval is a precondition for submission, so plan at least two rounds of advisor review before the deadline.
- After completion, the final thesis is submitted as a PDF to the library's thesis submission system, whether or not it is made public (library thesis-submission pages).
- Plagiarism is defined as misconduct in the handbook; rules for generative AI in theses were not found in public documents. Agree on any use with the advisor.

Presentation lengths are set per program and year. A public Information Science area program for 2021 listed 20 minutes of presentation and 10 minutes of questions per student; this is not evidence for other areas or years.

## Not confirmed from official sources

Secondary guides make claims such as specific talk/Q&A lengths for a program, failure rates, or area-specific criteria wording. Treat these as prompts to ask, not as rules, until an official source is found and recorded in the fact sheet.

## Historical field-specific document (do not treat as current)

The Materials Science area published a criteria PDF ([busitu_sinsa_kijun.pdf](https://www.naist.jp/publish/files/busitu_sinsa_kijun.pdf)) whose metadata dates to 2016 and whose title indicates a 2014/H27 update. It lists seven items, adds knowledge/literature and oral presentation, and describes separate 100-point scores for the thesis and for the presentation and Q&A, with 60 points required for each. Whether this applies to a current student is unconfirmed. Use it only as a reminder that the talk and the Q&A can be judged separately from the written thesis, so preparing the Q&A deserves the same weight as the thesis.

## NAIST-specific preparation notes

- Committees include members outside the student's subfield. Prepare non-specialist explanations of the background, the key method and the main figure.
- Confirm with the program office or advisor: presentation and Q&A lengths, slide and abstract templates, submission deadlines, language, and whether a lab-level rehearsal or advisor sign-off is required. Record each as confirmed (with source) or unconfirmed.
- Scores, pass predictions or "the committee will ask X" are never stated as facts.
